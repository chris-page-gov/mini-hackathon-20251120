# Insight / Narrative Prompt – Turn charts into a story

You are helping a team in a **mini data visualisation hackathon** turn their analysis into a clear story for non‑technical decision‑makers.

## Inputs

I will provide:

- The dataset and strategy name (e.g. “Digital Infrastructure – Connectivity vs Deprivation Map”).
- The SME question(s) we are trying to address.
- A short description of each chart and table we have created, including:
  - what is on each axis,
  - what the colours or categories represent,
  - any key numbers we want to highlight.

## Tasks

1. **Summarise the findings (facts)**  
   - Describe what the charts and tables *actually show* without adding interpretation.  
   - Highlight:
     - the strongest patterns,
     - any outliers,
     - any uncertainties or data gaps.

2. **Interpret the findings (your analysis)**  
   - Explain what these patterns could mean for policy or decision‑making.  
   - Be explicit that this is interpretation, not raw fact.  
   - Offer 2–4 plausible explanations where there is ambiguity.

3. **Answer the SME question(s)**  
   - Link the findings back directly to the question(s) we started with.  
   - If the data cannot fully answer a question, say so and explain why.

4. **Propose talking points and “so what?” messages**  
   - Generate:
     - a 1–2 sentence **headline message**,  
     - 3–5 bullet‑point **key messages**,  
     - 2–3 **questions or next steps** for decision‑makers.

5. **Flag limitations and caveats**  
   - Note any important limitations (coverage, data quality, modelling assumptions).  
   - Suggest what extra data or analysis would strengthen the case.

## Style and constraints

- Use British English.
- Keep the tone clear, honest and non‑sensational.
- Assume the audience understands basic percentages and rankings, but not statistical jargon.
- Use short paragraphs and bullet points.

Do **not** write any code. Focus entirely on explanation and storytelling.
